What is the SIR model?

The SIR model is a compartmental model used in epidemiology to predict how an infectious disease spreads through a population. The population is divided into three groups whose sizes change over time t:

  • Susceptible (S): healthy people who don’t have the disease yet and can catch it.
  • Infected (I): people who have the disease and can pass it on.
  • Recovered (R): people who have recovered, can no longer pass it on, and are immune.

The rate equations

People move between the groups at these rates:

\[ \begin{aligned} S' &= -a\,S\,I \\ I' &= a\,S\,I - b\,I \\ R' &= b\,I \end{aligned} \]

Model parameters

Transmission coefficient (a): how contagious the disease is, per contact between a susceptible and an infected person. The program uses a = 0.00001.

Recovery coefficient (b): the fraction of infected people who recover each day. It equals 1/d, where d is the average length of the illness in days. The program uses b = 1 / 14, an illness lasting about two weeks.

Euler’s method

The program can’t solve the equations exactly, so it takes small steps of length \(\Delta t\). At each step it computes the rates, multiplies by \(\Delta t\), and adds the changes:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta S &= S'\,\Delta t, & S_{\text{new}} &= S + \Delta S \\ \Delta I &= I'\,\Delta t, & I_{\text{new}} &= I + \Delta I \\ \Delta R &= R'\,\Delta t, & R_{\text{new}} &= R + \Delta R \\ && t_{\text{new}} &= t + \Delta t \end{aligned} \]

Try it: change the parameters

Move the sliders to change the model. This graph runs the same Euler steps as the program below, so it updates instantly. Hover over the graph to read the values.

R starts at 2,500, as in the program. With a large step size, Euler's method can overshoot, and the values can even go below zero.
